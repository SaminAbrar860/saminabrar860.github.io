Does Working From Home Improve Productivity?

Individual academic case study · People Analytics HR AnalyticsStatistical Modelling
Work autonomy was the strongest driver of both motivation and productivity, and its effect on productivity runs partly through increased motivation. Flexibility, on its own, made no measurable difference.
Project overview

This case study looks at 90 remote call-centre employees to understand what actually drives motivation and productivity while teleworking — and tests not just whether factors matter, but how they connect to one another.

Business problem / research question

Among remote employees, what really drives productivity: autonomy, organisational support, or scheduling flexibility — and does motivation explain part of that relationship?

Project classification

Individual academic case study.

My contribution

Completed independently as an individual case study.

Approach
  • Ran descriptive statistics and correlation analysis across autonomy, organisational support, flexibility, motivation, and productivity
  • Built two multiple regression models — one predicting motivation, one predicting productivity
  • Ran a formal mediation analysis (Baron & Kenny method) to test whether motivation explains part of autonomy's effect on productivity
  • Separately addressed the research ethics of the scenario: pressure to produce favourable findings, rushed timelines, and employee data confidentiality
Tools & methods

SPSS · multiple regression · Baron & Kenny mediation analysis

Key findings
  • Work autonomy was the strongest predictor of both motivation (β = .650, p < .001) and productivity (β = .413, p < .001)
  • Organisational support significantly boosted motivation, but not productivity directly
  • Work flexibility was not a significant predictor of either outcome
  • Motivation partially mediates the link between autonomy and productivity — autonomy helps both directly and by making people more motivated
Business relevance

Suggests organisations designing remote-work policy should prioritise employee autonomy and genuine support over flexible scheduling alone.

Limitations

Self-reported survey data from a single call-centre context; findings may not generalise to other industries.
