Scripted vs. GenAI Chatbot — South Australia Tourism Assistant

Group academic project (5 members) · Chatbots & Text Analytics Applied AIText Analytics
Tested with 66 real participants (132 evaluations): the GenAI chatbot won on satisfaction, accuracy, and understanding unexpected questions; the scripted bot was marginally faster and more consistent.
Project overview

Most discussions about scripted vs. AI chatbots are theoretical. My team built two fully working chatbots — one rule-based, one powered by an LLM — and tested them with real people to see which one actually performs better for tourists visiting South Australia.

Business problem / research question

Does a generative-AI chatbot genuinely outperform a traditional rule-based one for real users seeking tourism information, or does the simpler option hold its own?

Project classification

Group academic project (5 members).

My contribution

Contributed across the project lifecycle as part of a 5-person team effort, including chatbot design and the Python-based analysis of participant feedback.

Approach
  • Built two working chatbots in Voiceflow — one rule-based, one powered by an LLM with a grounded knowledge base — covering the same six tourism categories
  • Recruited 66 participants to test both bots on identical tasks and complete an evaluation survey
  • Analysed quantitative ratings (task completion, accuracy, ease of use, satisfaction) and qualitative comments using Python (pandas, NLTK, spaCy, word clouds)
Tools & methods

Voiceflow · Python (pandas, NLTK, spaCy, WordCloud) · survey design · descriptive statistics

Key findings
  • GenAI chatbot scored higher on task completion (4.05 vs 3.92), accuracy (4.08 vs 4.02), understanding unexpected questions (4.03 vs 3.67), and overall satisfaction (4.12 vs 3.95)
  • The scripted chatbot was marginally better on relevance (4.09 vs 4.02) and response speed (3.53s vs 3.58s)
Business relevance

Suggests tourism operators don't have to choose one architecture outright — a hybrid approach can offer GenAI's flexibility while keeping a scripted fallback for reliability-critical information.

Limitations

Sample was recruited by convenience, not randomly; results may not generalise beyond this specific tourism use case.
