Predicting M&A Acquisition Targets

Individual academic project · Predictive & Visual Analytics FinanceMachine Learning
Built a logistic regression model on real firm-level data (2012–2022) showing that profitability, cash reserves, debt, and R&D spending — not firm size or location — are the reliable signals.
Project overview

Written from the perspective of a hedge-fund analyst, this project asks which financial characteristics make a U.S. firm more likely to become a merger-and-acquisition target — using real, large-scale financial data rather than a toy dataset.

Business problem / research question

Which firm-level financial characteristics predict the likelihood of a company becoming an M&A target, and can this inform earlier, more confident investment screening?

Project classification

Individual academic project.

My contribution

Completed independently as an individual assignment.

Approach
  • Merged two large real datasets: 159,032 rows of firm-level financials and 33,436 rows of historical M&A targets (2012–2022)
  • Handled the ~13% missing data using an Iterative Imputer, which models each variable from the others rather than dropping rows
  • Validated the stability of summary statistics using bootstrapping (1,000 resamples)
  • Trimmed extreme outliers using percentile-based thresholds, reducing the working dataset from ~159,000 to ~38,000 rows for a cleaner analytical panel
  • Built three progressively refined logistic regression models, adding industry and year fixed effects
Tools & methods

Python (pandas, scikit-learn, statsmodels) · logistic regression · iterative imputation · bootstrapping

Key findings
  • Profitability, debt levels, cash reserves, and R&D spending were reliable predictors of becoming an acquisition target
  • Sales growth and firm location (including California's tech hubs) were not reliable predictors, contrary to some conventional assumptions
Business relevance

Gives an investment team a data-driven starting point for screening potential acquisition targets, to be paired with qualitative judgement rather than used alone.

Limitations

The sample period (2012–2022) may not reflect current market conditions; the model identifies correlation, not intent to sell.
