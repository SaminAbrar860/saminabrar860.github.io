E-Commerce Customer, Channel & Campaign Analytics

Group academic project (4 members) · Customer Analytics Marketing AnalyticsDashboarding
Identified Paid Search as the highest-value channel and "Ongoing Specials" as the most cost-efficient campaign, while flagging Organic Search as an underused growth opportunity.
Project overview

Given a full year of e-commerce traffic and campaign data, this project set out to answer a very practical marketing question: which channels and campaigns actually deserve more budget?

Business problem / research question

Which marketing channels and campaigns delivered the strongest customer and business outcomes, and where should future spend be concentrated?

Project classification

Group academic project (4 members).

My contribution

Contributed across the project lifecycle as part of a 4-person team effort.

Approach
  • Analysed a full year (2023) of e-commerce traffic, channel, and campaign data
  • Built KPI scorecards, channel comparisons, and campaign breakdowns in Google Looker Studio
  • Benchmarked campaigns on cost-per-order (CPO) against a target threshold
Tools & methods

Google Sheets, Google Looker Studio · KPI scorecards · channel & campaign performance analysis

Key findings
  • Paid Search had the highest average order value ($112.84); Direct traffic drove the highest volume (644,887 users, $1.88M revenue)
  • Organic Search was flagged as an underexplored growth opportunity
  • "Ongoing Specials" was the most cost-efficient campaign (lowest cost-per-order at $35.38, 51.2% share of completed orders), while "Seasonal Savings" had high visibility but weaker conversion, exceeding the CPO benchmark
Business relevance

Gives a marketing team a clear basis for reallocating budget toward higher-converting channels and campaigns rather than spreading spend evenly.

Limitations

Analysis is descriptive, not causal — it identifies correlation between channels/campaigns and outcomes, not proof that reallocating budget would reproduce the same results.
